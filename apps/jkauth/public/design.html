01IDENTITY

One identity, two faces

jkOS is skeuomorphic, not flat-SaaS. A single attribute — data-mode on <html> — switches between two physical worlds. There are exactly two values, "paper" and "dark"; the mode is controlled explicitly and never from prefers-color-scheme. Corners are soft by default (sharp is a per-app or per-card opt-in through the --hub-radius* tokens); chrome is built from CSS hardware, spent sparingly.

Drag the divider — one specimen markup, paper physics left, CRT physics right

data-mode="paper"

Warm kraft-paper office — tan/cream layers, dark ink, brass hardware, film-grain multiply, burnt-sienna default accent. Accents work as wells and badges — sunk boundaries and raised emphasis (§05).

data-mode="dark"

CRT amber-phosphor terminal — near-black warm grounds, #ffb000 amber, text glow, scanline + vignette veils. Accents emit — halation is the dark face's badge (§08).

02ACCENT CHAIN

The two-accent chain

Both accents are user-driven and co-equal — a vivid primary and a vivid secondary, never a neutral. Apps supply only a default pair; the live pair is the signed-in user's. From two raw inputs the whole palette derives: paper deepens them toward ink for legibility, dark keeps them raw and adds glow. Never hardcode --accent — let it derive; components read the --color-accent* aliases.

the fourth weight is the tapered rule — same ink as the three above, and the only one that dies into the ground at both ends instead of being cut square. That is the whole of it. A square cut is right inside something that already has an edge; at the boundaries of the canvas there is no edge, so a square-cut rule terminates in mid-air and the page reads as cropped. This weight was briefly given a material — a hammered-steel bead on paper, a lit silver blade on the tube — on the theory that the one line with its own silhouette had earned one. In place it read as a strip of trim bolted across the page, louder than the masthead it was bounding. Deleted; the taper stayed. It is spent on the masthead's edge (§11) and nowhere else; the canvas foot borrows the same geometry. Inside a bordered container keep .jk-rule — a taper there is fussiness.

05WELLS · BADGES

Wells & badges — the paper accent language

Paper mode has exactly two physical accent moves. The well sinks into the sheet — an inner shadow that gives a region depth; wells provide boundaries (contain, group, delimit). The badge sits on the sheet — a raised chip with a drop shadow, put on text and small marks; the badge is paper's emphasis, the exact analogue of dark mode's glow (§08). Primary accent takes the badge treatment; secondary stays flat, one rung down, never raised. In CRT both moves go emissive automatically. Use the @jkos/ui React wrappers (<Press>, <Bubble>, <Sub>, <Well>, <Sheet>, <Pill>, <TButton>) — don't recreate them.

The chip — the solid-ink tab (Full Press, the suite default item)

A chip is a single tinted item — a calendar event, a task leaf, a bench card. The default .jk-chip.jk-chip-solid is a saturated --jk-tint fill with the type cream-knocked-out and pressed in (.jk-press-rev); the faint .jk-chip base keeps neutral ink (.jk-press-ink). It mode-flips like every accent surface — inset bevel + drop on paper, halation ring on the tube. Supersedes the calendar kit's old ACCENT_GLAZE look. React: <Chip> + <Press variant="rev|ink|sm">.

The four states are not a style choice per call site — chipState(item, now) in @jkos/cards decides which one an item is wearing, so a chip carries the same weight in every view that renders it. Spent is the state that is easy to forget: ended, but nobody struck it off. It keeps its ink and loses only its weight.

06CONTROLS

Fields, toggles, faders & meters

One rule across the set: a neutral, debossed track or slot that fills with the accent (or --jk-tint) as it engages. Two halves — fields take ink, toggles hold state. React: <Field>, <NumField>, <SelectField>, <TextArea>, <Switch>, <Check>, <Slider>, <VU> — all on real form/aria hosts, so the platform keeps the keyboard and the state styling keys off the aria attribute.

jk-field — the write half

The recess is a token pair, not a shared shadow: paper debosses (--hub-field-recess — shadow off the top lip, light caught at the bottom), the tube drops to unlit --hub-screen with an inner falloff and no bevel at all, then emits on focus. Flip the face above and watch the same markup change physics rather than palette. Every native part is off — appearance: none, the white spin buttons, the OS select arrow, autofill yellow, the search ×.

08GLOW · HALATION

Phosphor glow & halation

Glow is the dark face's badge: where paper raises a chip off the sheet (§05), the CRT lights the phosphor behind it — same emphasis, opposite physics, same scarcity rules. It is mode-gated and only lights up in dark mode; on paper the same accent renders as the badge/well pair instead. Switch Face → Dark to see these come alive. .jk-halo / .jk-glow collapse to nothing on paper, so one class is correct in both faces.

11MEASURE

The canvas

One measure per page. A full-page view's outermost element is the canvas, and everything — rails included — lives inside it. Before this each view invented its own rule, so on a wide monitor one app centred its content while its own rail stayed welded to the window edge 700px away. The page owns the measure; a component fills what it is given. That is also why the @jkos/cards views carry no max-width: in a widget they fill the widget, on a page they fill the page.

Width is fluid with a cap — --jk-canvas takes the smallest of the window minus its gutters, 900px + 34vw, and --jk-canvas-cap. Below ~1440px the canvas simply fills; above it, it grows at about a third the rate the window does, so the margins open progressively instead of all at once, and stop at 1760. Roughly 964 @1024 · 1380 @1440 · 1545 @1920 · 1760 @2560 and up. A phone needs no second implementation: the first term wins outright and the gutter drops to zero.

A measure, not a card. The canvas draws nothing: the page ground is the app's own grained background and content sits directly on it. It was briefly drawn as a lifted sheet of paper stock on a darker desk — that read as a document floating in a window instead of as the app's ground, so it's gone, along with --jk-desk and .jk-canvas-sheet. Something that should read as a raised card uses .jk-sheet — at card scale, not page scale.

The head's edge is one hairline. .jk-masthead spends no fill and no border: the ground's grain runs unbroken from the first pixel, and the bottom edge is the tapered rule of §04, drawn from the same --jk-inlay-* seams so the two are one object rather than two that drift. It replaced an opaque bar with a square border-bottom, which was a slab: its fill was the one place in the app where the grain stopped, and because the canvas floats on the ground its edges were square cuts in mid-air. It then overcorrected twice — an ink-rule-plus-hairline ladder, then a metal bead — before landing where it should have started: one feathered hairline, quieter than the type above it. .jk-canvas-foot takes the same feathered margin, so head and foot bracket the page as a pair.

Nav in a masthead is boxed (.jk-masthead-tab, plus .jk-well on the current one). It spent a pass as a fill-less thumb-index — marked by weight and a stub of accent rule on the ladder — and came back, which is the instructive part: a filled box states the face instantly, because the fill IS the face (debossed tint on paper, emissive ring and glow on the tube, both from --hub-accent-press). A weight change and a 2px stub state neither, and the nav is the first thing looked at. Reuse the well; don't restate its mode flip.

RAIL

--jk-rail · a pane inside the canvas

the foot holds the bottom of the measure

The detail panel overlays the canvas — it is never a member of it. The pane that slides in over a page to show one selected thing, in two variants of one primitive: .jk-panel-rail pins to the content area's inboard right edge (soft on the two corners that face the content, square on the one it shares with the window); .jk-panel-sheet is the below-desktop bottom sheet. .jk-panel-head is the selected item's own flag — a solid --jk-tint band, so it reads as the loudest member of the chip family (§13) rather than as a coloured toolbar.

position: absolute here is load-bearing, not styling. This panel has broken twice, both times because it was written as a grid item sharing the app shell's content cell: once through a fill-moded transform making it a containing block for the fixed popups it hosted, and once because it was the only definitely-placed grid child — grid placed it first, auto-placement then found its cell taken, pushed <main> into an implicit row, and the declared 1fr row collapsed to 0px. The panel mounted, rendered its whole subtree, measured zero, and vanished in every view at once. Give it a positioned, pointer-events: none host spanning the region it covers — and never grid-row, grid-column or a self-alignment again. (pnpm check:overlay holds this.)

jk-cards-* — the @jkos/cards interaction affordances

The calendar kit carries its own hover/press transitions so they render wherever it mounts (BeigeBoard tabs and ORDECK widgets) instead of depending on a host app.css only one app ships. They are transitions over the host's shape — hover a row, press a button.

jk-cards-row — hover me CHIP
jk-cards-row — another CHIP

.jk-hit is the same idea one level down: the hover response for anything clickable that has no button face — a goal card, a calendar cell, a caret. It lifts the background and takes a 1px press, and that is deliberately all it does. .jk-scroll is the scroll region, vertical only, so a long title can never shove a pane sideways. .jk-divider is .jk-rule's vertical sibling — the hairline that separates clusters inside a bar.

The scroll mark is a hairline, not a gutter: 2px of ink on the stock, no track, no channel, no arrows. It is stated on the bare pseudo-elements so it dresses every scroller in the suite — .jk-scroll carries no bar styling of its own, and a pane that forgot the class still gets the mark. Three rungs, all tokens: --hub-scroll-mark-rest is always painted, because a bar you cannot see is a bar whose position you cannot read; --hub-scroll-mark deepens it while the pointer is inside the pane or the pane is moving; --hub-scroll-mark-hover brightens it with the pointer on the mark itself, and a drag goes full accent. The "moving" half is the one rung CSS cannot reach — a wheel fling past the pane fires no :hover — so installScrollHairline() stamps data-scrolling from one capture-phase listener; injectJkOSTheme() already calls it. 15MOTION

Motion vocabulary

Ambient hardware effects (LED pulse, occasional flicker) are the house idiom — subtle, opacity-only. Entrances land in ~200–400ms. Adjust CRT intensity via the --crt-* tokens, never by baking overlay opacity into a component. Everything below honours prefers-reduced-motion. Hover a tile (or tap it) and its entrance replays on a loop — with a rest beat between plays, so it demonstrates rather than strobes.

16FOR A DESIGN PASS

The fence — elevate inside these lines

The invariants a design pass must not cross. Push craft on spacing, hierarchy, composition and atmosphere; keep the token names, the two-mode contract and the derivation chain intact.

  • DOExtend the retro-hardware language — LEDs, tape, stamps, perf, 7-seg, screens — as scarce punctuation.
  • DOResolve every colour through the --hub-* / --color-* chain.
  • DOReuse the shared classes & @jkos/ui components; make both faces hold up.
  • DOAccent with the paper pair — wells for boundary, badges for emphasis (glow in CRT).
  • DOFade colours with withAlpha(), never hex-concat on a CSS var.
  • DON'TRename any --hub-* token, or hardcode --accent / a hex in a component.
  • DON'TUse prefers-color-scheme — mode is driven by data-mode.
  • DON'TScatter hardware chrome decoratively — an LED on everything means nothing is live.
  • DON'TAdd an icon library, or normalise the chrome toward flat modern UI.
  • DON'TRestate the derivation or re-set a CRT knob per app — inputs only.
